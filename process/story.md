# How I work

Every project is different, but most of the time it follows five steps. In the beginning we have a free interview, so you can see exactly what you're getting into before you commit to anything.

## 1. Initial interview (free)

We talk through what you need, and I outline several options at different sizes. A logo can stand alone or grow into a full identity with guidlines of colors, typography and the wider visual system around it. 

A typeface can be a single weight or a full family with a wide range of characters, and some projects also need technical work so the font behaves properly on websites and apps. We choose the option that best fits your goals and budget.

At this step we agree who holds which rights to the work, meaning who owns what and how it may be used. And we choose a payment structure that suits you. Most often it's 30% after research, 40% after sketches, and 30% after final artwork and presentation. If it does not suit you we can always discuss alternatives

## 2. Proposal

You receive a written proposal with the scope, timeline, cost and agreed rights. Every project needs a different amount of time, so each proposal sets its own schedule. Paid work begins once you've approved it.

## 3. Research

I look at where your design and strategy stand today, research your competitors and the possible solutions, and gather visual and conceptual references to set the direction before any design begins.

## 4. Sketches

I start with rough, hand-drawn sketches. They're quick and open-ended, which lets us explore many directions and pick the right one before any time goes into polish.

## 5. Final artwork and handover

The chosen direction is built as clean digital artwork that stays sharp at any size, from a business card to a billboard. I present the result and hand over organized final files, ready to use. I'm available for questions for 30 days after handover.

You're involved at three points: after the research, after the sketches, and after the final artwork. Each phase includes two structured rounds of feedback, unless stated otherwise.
